Experiment 1

Endings

Exploring the art of the final page.

The experiment

Every reader has a relationship with the last page. Some of us rush toward it; others slow down and put the book away with a few chapters left, unwilling to say goodbye. In this first experiment, we look closely at how stories end, and what an ending asks of the person holding the book.

Over several weeks we read, argue and listen: to endings that close every door, endings that leave one open, and endings that change what came before. Placeholder: add anything here about the readings, the format and who the experiment is for.

Come with an opinion, or come to lose one.

Prompts

  1. Do you remember the last line of the last book you finished?
  2. Is an ending a promise made on page one, or a surprise the book keeps hidden?
  3. What is the difference between a book that ends and a book that simply stops?
  4. Have you ever refused to finish a book you loved? What were you protecting?
  5. Which ending has stayed with you longest, and what would change if the last page were torn out?
  6. Should an ending resolve everything, or leave the door open?
  7. If your own life were a book, where would you want the last chapter to begin?
