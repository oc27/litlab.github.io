The experiment

How should we end things? From Tarantino to Wild West films and Hamlet, things end with bullets, swords, poison and a pile of bodies.

In contrast to thinking of an ending as a termination, we might consider that 'in my end is my beginning', as TS Eliot write in Four Quartets.

From the ways that narrative generates a sense of an ending, to the notorious proclamation that 'it is easier to imagine the end of the world than the end of capitalism', this experiment invites us to think about how, from the political to the personal, endings shape us.

What would you end right now?

The First Lit Lab Hangout

We were all set to go. A beautiful National Trust building in the heart of Hackney - Sutton House - had invited us to use their space. We had a structure and we had a bunch of people, mostly recruited from the London School of Solarpunk, ready to start a new discussion around the literary.

But then we lost access to the venue. An ending before we started. For reasons unclear, Sutton House had to shut its doors to community events for the foreseeable future. But as a grassroots education project, we were not fussed and instead invited everyone to hang out at the nearby graveyard. What better setting for the theme of 'endings'?

Nida Sajid presented some slides (now printed out and handed around) that offered possible trajectories for thinking about endings. Highlights were Francis Fukuyama's mistaken claim that we had reached the 'End of History' and the Tibetan monks who make and sweep away a sand mandala in the opening scene of the film Samsara.
